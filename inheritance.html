Crypto inheritance planning and executor support

If something happened to you tomorrow, could your family find your crypto, and use it, without anyone else being able to? KeyCompass makes sure the answer is yes, then helps your executors or attorneys when the time comes.

Short answer

Crypto you hold yourself does not pass on by itself. There is no bank to call and no password reset: if your family cannot find your recovery phrase and understand what to do with it, the crypto is lost. KeyCompass helps in three ways. Estate readiness tests that your setup could be recovered and gives your family plain-English instructions, from £495. Executor support guides your executors through recovering it after a death. Attorney support does the same for someone acting under a power of attorney. KeyCompass never holds a key, sees your recovery phrase or touches your funds.

Why crypto inheritance is different

With a bank account, your executors can show a death certificate and the bank does the rest. With crypto you hold yourself, nobody can do that for them. Whoever has the recovery phrase has the crypto, and whoever doesn't, doesn't.

That creates two risks that pull in opposite directions:

  • Too hidden, and your family may never find it, or may not know what it is when they do.
  • Too obvious, and someone else may find it first, while you are still alive.

A good plan sits between the two, and it gets harder with a passphrase or a multisig setup (several keys, with a set number needed to move funds), where your family needs more than one piece and a clear map of where each one is.

How it stays safe

The one rule

KeyCompass never holds a key, never sees a recovery phrase and never moves funds. You, and later your executors or attorneys, do every step on your own devices.

  • Your instruction pack stays with you. KeyCompass keeps a record that the work was done, but no copy of where your backups are kept, so there is nothing here worth stealing.
  • Authority is checked first. Nobody is helped as an executor or attorney until their grant of probate or power of attorney, and their ID, have been checked.
  • The pack doesn't depend on KeyCompass. It names "KeyCompass or another independent specialist", and it is written so that any competent person could follow it.

UK points to get right

Never put your recovery phrase in your will

In England and Wales, a will becomes a public document once probate is granted, and anyone can order a copy. Your will, or a separate letter of wishes, can say that you hold crypto and where instructions are kept, without the words themselves.

  • Crypto is property, and part of your estate. The Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 confirmed this in England, Wales and Northern Ireland from December 2025.
  • HMRC expects it on the inheritance tax return. Since January 2026 it has been writing to agents to remind them that crypto must be included, valued at the date of death.
  • Tell your solicitor you hold crypto. Executors need to know it exists to include it.
  • Exchange accounts are different. Crypto held on an exchange is handled through the exchange's own process for bereaved families, more like a bank account, but your executor still needs to know the account exists.

Common questions

Can my solicitor hold my recovery phrase? Some people leave sealed instructions with a solicitor. Leaving the recovery phrase itself with anyone puts your crypto in their hands, so think carefully, and never leave it with someone who doesn't understand what it is.

What if you're not around when my executors need help? The instruction pack is written so that any competent specialist could follow it, and it says so. Your plan never depends on KeyCompass still trading.

Do I have to decide now who helps my executors? No. Naming KeyCompass in your pack isn't a booking. Your executors decide when the time comes, and are quoted then.

What if I use a passphrase? Then your family needs both the recovery phrase and the passphrase. Without the passphrase, the recovery phrase alone opens a different wallet, not the one your crypto is in.

Someone has died and we've found a device or a list of words. Can you help? Yes. Book the executor triage call first, and don't type the words into anything, or reply to anyone who contacts you offering to recover the funds.
