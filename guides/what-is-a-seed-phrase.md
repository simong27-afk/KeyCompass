# What is a seed phrase?

> A seed phrase is the master key to a crypto wallet. Whoever has the words has the money.
> Here is what it is, how it works, and how to keep it out of the wrong hands.

**The short answer.** A seed phrase is a list of 12 or 24 ordinary words, created when you first set up your wallet, that can rebuild the whole wallet on any compatible device. Every
private key and every address in the wallet comes from those words. If your hardware wallet (the physical device that keeps your keys offline) is lost, broken or stolen, the seed phrase is how you get your crypto back. And if anyone else sees
it, they can take everything, from anywhere in the world, without needing your device or your PIN.

It goes by several names: seed phrase, recovery phrase, recovery seed, mnemonic or mnemonic phrase, backup phrase,
wallet backup, or Secret Recovery Phrase in MetaMask. They all mean the same thing.

## What a seed phrase looks like

A seed phrase is a numbered list of short, common English words, such as *ocean*, *ladder*,
*cabin*, *rescue*. Your device shows them to you once, during setup, and asks you to write them
down in order.

The words come from a fixed list of 2,048, published as part of a standard called BIP39 that
almost every wallet uses. Each word on the list can be identified by its first four letters,
which is why some metal backup plates only have room for four letters per word. The final word
also works as a checksum: it lets a wallet spot most typing mistakes rather than silently opening
the wrong wallet.

## How a seed phrase works

Behind the words is a very large random number, generated by your device. The words are simply a human-readable way of writing that number down, so a person can read and copy it without mistakes.

From that one number, the device works out a master key, and from the master key it works out every private key and address your wallet will ever use, for every coin and account. The same
words always produce the same keys, on any device or wallet app that follows the same standard. That is what
makes recovery possible.

Two things follow from this:

- **Your crypto is not stored on your device.** It is recorded on the blockchain. The
  device holds the keys that let you move it, and the seed phrase can recreate those keys. Lose
  the device and keep the phrase, and you have lost nothing but the device.
- **One seed phrase covers everything in that wallet.** You do not get a separate phrase for each
  coin or each account. Bitcoin, Ethereum and the rest on one device all come from the
  same words.

## Seed phrase, private key, password and PIN: the differences

| | What it is | What it can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Seed phrase** | 12 to 24 words, created once at setup | Rebuilds the entire wallet, every coin and account, on any compatible device |
| **Private key** | A long code derived from the seed phrase | Controls one address. Your device and wallet app handle these for you; you rarely see them |
| **Device PIN** | A number you choose | Unlocks your hardware device. Useless without the device itself |
| **App or exchange password** | A password you choose | Opens an app or an exchange account. Does not control crypto secured by a hardware device |

The PIN protects the device. The seed phrase *is* the wallet. That is why someone who steals your device usually cannot get in, but someone who sees your seed phrase can.

## 12, 18, 20 or 24 words

Most devices and wallet apps use 12 or 24 words, and a few offer 18. Both 12 and 24 are far beyond what anyone
could guess: a 12-word phrase is one of roughly 340 trillion trillion trillion possibilities. A
longer phrase adds a safety margin, not a practical difference for most people. What matters far
more is how the phrase is stored.

Newer Trezor devices create a 20-word backup by default. It uses a different standard, called
SLIP39, from a different word list. It does the same job, and several other wallet apps support it,
but it is not interchangeable with a 12 or 24-word phrase. If yours has 20 words, that is
expected.

## Where your seed phrase comes from

Your device generates the seed phrase itself when you set it up. That is the only
safe way to get one.

- **Never accept a seed phrase you did not watch your own device create.** A device that arrives with words already printed on a card, or "pre-set up" by a seller, is a scam: whoever
  wrote those words can empty the wallet whenever they like.
- **Never use an online seed phrase generator** for a wallet that will hold real money. Any words
  that have appeared on a website or a computer screen could have been recorded.
- **Never make up your own words.** People are poor at choosing randomly, and phrases chosen by
  people can be guessed.

## If your crypto is on an exchange

If you hold crypto on an exchange such as Coinbase or Kraken, you do not have a seed phrase. The
exchange holds the keys and you have an account, protected by a password and two-factor
authentication. That is what "not your keys, not your coins" refers to.

You get a seed phrase when you move your crypto into a wallet you control. See
[How to move crypto from an exchange to a hardware wallet](https://keycompass.co.uk/guides/move-crypto-from-exchange-to-hardware-wallet).

## Storing your seed phrase safely: the rules

The short version is below. For paper versus metal, where to keep it and how many copies to make, see
[Seed phrase storage: where should I keep mine?](https://keycompass.co.uk/guides/seed-phrase-storage)

- **Only ever enter it on the device itself,** and only to recover or check the wallet.
  Never type it into a phone, a computer, a website, an app, a password manager or an email.
- **Never photograph it, scan it or store it in the cloud.** Photos sync to the cloud
  automatically, and cloud accounts get hacked.
- **Nobody legitimate will ever ask for it.** Not Ledger, not Trezor, not your exchange, not
  "support", and not KeyCompass. Anyone who asks for it is trying to steal from you.
- **Write it down clearly and in order,** and keep the copy away from the device. Someone who
  finds both together has everything.
- **Protect it from fire, flood and the people who share your home.** Paper is fine to start
  with; stamped or engraved metal survives far more.
- **Make sure the right person could find it,** and know what it is for, if something happened
  to you.
- **Check it.** A backup with one wrong word can fail when you need it. See
  [How to test your recovery phrase backup safely](https://keycompass.co.uk/guides/test-recovery-phrase-backup).

## The optional passphrase

Some devices and wallet apps let you add a passphrase on top of the seed phrase, sometimes called a 25th word.
The same seed phrase with a different passphrase opens a completely separate wallet. This protects
you if someone finds your seed phrase: on its own, it opens only the wallet without the
passphrase, which can be left empty or hold a small amount.

The catch is that the passphrase becomes a second thing you cannot lose. Forget it, or record it
wrongly by a single character, and the wallet behind it is gone, even with the seed phrase in
hand. It suits some people well, but it is worth understanding fully before you add one.

## Scams that target seed phrases

Almost every seed phrase scam tries to get you to type your words somewhere other than your device.

- **Fake support.** Someone replies to a post or messages you, claiming to be from a wallet
  maker or an exchange, and asks you to "verify", "sync" or "validate" your wallet. Real support
  never asks for your words and never contacts you first on social media.
- **Fake apps and websites.** Lookalike versions of wallet apps appear in search ads and app
  stores, and ask for your seed phrase to "restore" your wallet. Download wallet software only
  from the maker's own website, typed in yourself.
- **Letters and emails claiming a security problem.** Some are well printed and carry the right
  logo. Any message that ends with entering your words online is a scam.
- **The "free seed phrase".** Scammers post a seed phrase in public comments, posing as a
  beginner who cannot work out how to withdraw. The wallet holds money but cannot be emptied
  without a small fee, and anything you send to cover the fee is taken straight away. And a wallet
  that is not yours is not yours to empty, whatever the comment says.
- **"Recovery services".** Anyone offering to recover lost crypto in exchange for a fee up front,
  or for your seed phrase, is a scam.

## Common questions

**Can someone guess my seed phrase?** No. The number of possible phrases is so large that guessing
one is not realistic with any computer that exists. Seed phrases are stolen, not guessed: through
scams, photos, cloud backups and careless storage.

**Does the order of the words matter?** Yes. The same words in a different order make a different
phrase, which is why your backup should be numbered.

**Can I change my seed phrase?** Not on an existing wallet. If you think your phrase has been seen
by anyone, set up a new wallet with a new phrase, test the backup, and move your crypto across.
Then treat the old phrase as compromised.

**I've lost my seed phrase but still have the device. What now?** Your crypto is safe for now,
but the device is your only way in. Do not reset it. Set up a new wallet with a new phrase and
move your funds across while the old device still works. See
[I've lost my recovery phrase. What now?](https://keycompass.co.uk/lost-recovery-phrase)

**Can I use my seed phrase with a different device or app?** Usually, yes, as long as both follow the same
standard. Some wallet apps organise accounts slightly differently, so balances do not always appear
straight away after a restore. Recover onto another hardware device, never into a phone or desktop
app just to see if it works.

**Is it safe to keep it in a password manager?** It moves your seed phrase onto internet-connected
devices, which is exactly what a hardware device exists to avoid. Keep it offline.
